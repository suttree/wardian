# Narrative Layer

The Living Cabinet can belong to the same imaginative universe as The Corpus.

In that reading, the cabinet is not only a plant display. It is an Archivist's desk: a place where each living specimen is catalogued, observed, and preserved in domestic ritual.

Each vessel can have:

- an accession number
- provenance
- handwritten notes
- seasonal observations
- care records
- specimen rotations
- small stories or fragments

This gives the project a layer beyond interior design. The cabinet becomes a living archive, with each plant treated as a specimen in a private collection.

## Accession Format

A simple accession number can make each specimen feel part of a larger collection.

Example:

```text
LC-001
Coleus scutellarioides
Introduced: 2026-07-04
Source: TBD
```

## Tone

The narrative should remain quiet. It should deepen the object without making it theatrical in an obvious way.

Avoid heavy worldbuilding on the surface of the cabinet. Let the story appear through labels, notes, records, provenance, and repeated care.

## Design Implications

- Labels can use accession numbers as well as plant names.
- Observation logs can read like specimen records.
- Photos can become part of an archive rather than just documentation.
- Future interfaces can behave like catalogues, not dashboards.
- The cabinet can evolve into a working collection with its own history.
