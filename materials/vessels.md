# Vessels

The vessel is the central display object. It should read as laboratory glass, apothecary jar, or specimen container rather than plant pot.

## Desired Qualities

- clear glass
- stable base
- wide enough opening for cleaning
- simple silhouette
- compatible with a plant support or collar
- visually balanced with a single compact plant
- beautiful even when empty

## Candidate Types

## Borosilicate Beaker

Pros:

- Strong laboratory association.
- Easy to source.
- Good visibility.

Cons:

- May feel too obviously like a beaker.
- Pour spout can disrupt the museum-object feeling.

## Apothecary Jar

Pros:

- Strong cabinet-of-curiosities association.
- More domestic and antique feeling.

Cons:

- Lid may complicate plant support.
- Some shapes are hard to clean.

## Tissue Culture Jar

Pros:

- Excellent scientific-display language.
- Compact and specimen-like.

Cons:

- May be too small.
- Could feel clinical without warm material support.

## Hydroponic Propagation Vase

Pros:

- Already designed for roots and water.
- Often visually elegant.

Cons:

- Some designs lean decorative rather than museum-like.

## Open Design Problem: Algae

Clear glass makes roots and water visible, but it also exposes the nutrient solution to light. Early prototypes should test how quickly algae appears and whether the display can remain beautiful with partial shading, careful placement, lower light spill, amber glass, or a removable sleeve.
