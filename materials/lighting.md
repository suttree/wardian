# Lighting

Lighting is probably the most important material in the project.

The light should make the specimen feel chosen, examined, and quietly important. It should not make the room feel like a grow tent.

## Desired Qualities

- warm-white appearance
- full-spectrum plant support
- focused downward beam
- discreet fixture
- hidden or well-managed cable
- stable timer support
- no visible purple cast

## Candidate Approaches

## Clamp-On Grow Light

Pros:

- Easy first test.
- Often includes timer and dimming.
- Minimal build effort.

Cons:

- Can look like a consumer gadget.
- Cable and clamp may dominate the object.

## Magnetic Shelf Spotlight

Pros:

- Strong museum-display feeling.
- Can disappear under a shelf.

Cons:

- Needs suitable mounting surface.
- Plant performance depends on spectrum and output.

## Pendant Grow Bulb

Pros:

- Simple and theatrical.
- Can create strong focus.

Cons:

- Fixture may become visually dominant.
- Harder to integrate quietly without a cabinet.

## Hidden LED Strip

Pros:

- Easy to conceal.
- Good for future multi-shelf cabinet.

Cons:

- Less focused than a spotlight.
- Can feel architectural rather than specimen-specific.

## First Prototype Notes

For Prototype 001, choose something easy to move and adjust. The goal is to learn the relationship between light height, beam spread, plant health, algae, and atmosphere before designing permanent lighting.
